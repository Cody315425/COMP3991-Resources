Welcome!¶

I made this site to catalog the various resources for COMP3991. Where possible I will provide some explanations for the concepts but note that I may still be trying to wrap my head around them myself :D.

Preface¶

This is the first time the course is being run, as such we really have no prior testing material to go off of with regards to questions. The course is heavily based on Mike Cohen's work on Calculus and Linear Algebra so we may assume that the examinations will be similar to his exercises. Dr. Manohar really likes proofs however so although it's an "Applied Math" course you should expect quite a few proofs.

For this guide I will be speaking from my own experience with math and will be showing my approach to this course. You do not need to follow my approach, however I hope the resources are helpful. If you have any additional resources you wish to share with the class, feel free to send them to me and I will update the page.

My Approach¶

Math is a very tricky subject, most people (me included) can’t just sit down and listen to math be explained. The key is repetition. When learning math I use an approach called the blackbox method. The short of that is this: “You do not need to understand how something works to use it.”

I tend to approach topics in math by solving the questions first then understanding why the method works later when I have more experience with it. The proof based approach taken in class is the opposite, we learn why things work before we use them.

The issue I have with this approach is that it overwhelms the listener with information. Proofs by their nature are very verbose and they describe things that the listener may have no experience at all working with.

We're mostly lucky in that this course is an applied math course so every concept we learn has a physical (or digital) impact that it can be related to. This greatly helps in forming that intuition for problem solving.

My advice, keep practicing questions, look at solutions, build a connection with the tools then try to understand them.
